## Measured

- **Contrast:** 18 pairs. 17 pass AA. One fails by rule: lichen `#C9D94E` as
  type is **1.40:1** on stock (3:1 floor for display, 4.5:1 for text), so it is a
  fill-only colour. The range accent (tone 5) is **4.82:1** light and **5.06:1**
  night, which is why it is the only tone allowed to set footer links, badges and
  the focus ring. Night body text is 16.21:1.
- **Tone ramp:** seven steps per theme. Night is a separate ramp
  (`#0A110D → #A6D2B0`) where near ridges get lighter; it is not the light ramp
  inverted.
- **Mark:** 64 grid. Flat bottom at `y57`, vertical sides at `x3` / `x61`, a
  top edge climbing `24` units over `58` — a 22° slope. The crest is a `7`-unit
  band cut from that edge, so the highest surface of the silhouette is the
  lichen. No channel inside the mass, and therefore no compact drawing: the
  same geometry is used from 16px to 512px.
- **The field:** two full-bleed dither exposures, hero (seed 7) and footer
  (seed 11), six ridges each, 2px cells, drawn by the same engine and measured
  at 1440 × 320 and 1440 × 240. Both dissolve into paper at the top and darken
  downward: hero row means 243 → 173 → 130, footer 243 → 171 → 145.
- **Page weight:** no images at runtime on the route; the hero, study and range
  thumb are canvas drawn by the same engine, and every mark is SVG.
- **Keyboard:** the skip link is the first tab stop; submitting the empty form
  moves focus to the `role="alert"` summary and marks three fields
  `aria-invalid="true"`; a valid submit replaces the form with a `role="status"`
  confirmation.

## Fixes made during this pass

1. **The first mark was a peak at 16px.** The ridge passed the 64px test and
   failed the only test that matters. A second pass drew six more marks; the H
   with printed feet replaced it. The first pass is kept as history in
   `explorations/directions.md`; the page's mark section now shows the second
   pass, including the control.
2. **The plate's fade ran the wrong way.** `drawRange` faded from the middle
   *down*, leaving the top of every plate empty and only the bottom 42% printed.
   It now dissolves upward into the paper, as the component does.
3. **The accent tone was under 4.5:1.** Light tone 5 moved `#5C7C55` → `#557347`
   (4.24 → 4.82), and the night ramp was retuned so tone 5 measures 5.06:1.
4. **The fusion rows overflowed.** The note column was `auto`, so long verdicts
   pushed the grid past its track. It is now a sized track that wraps, with the
   note left-aligned.
5. **The altimeter wrapped its unit.** `2,480 m` split across lines; the value is
   now `flex: none` and `nowrap`.
6. **One lint failure in the supplied component.** A ref was mutated during
   render (`opts.current = …`). Moved into an effect; behaviour is unchanged and
   the draw loop still reads the latest props each frame.
7. **“Save as draft” was a dead control.** It now writes the report to
   `localStorage` and restores it on the next visit; filing a report clears the
   draft. The note under the form is true.
8. **The H lost the v2.3 bake-off.** It passed 16px but never drew the slope.
   The mark is now four solid wedge-bars that thicken left to right, with a
   lichen apex; the compact drops to three bars. No circle, no stroke. The old
   foot-cell mark is history in `design/brand/hachure/explorations/directions.md`.

## v3.0 — the mark redrawn, the field promoted

Two changes, one argument: the case had a mark that drew a chart and a dither
that was framed as a picture. Both were the same mistake at different scales —
treating density as decoration instead of as the measurement.

### The mark

The v2.3 mark used **height** to mean rise: four bars getting taller. That is
the grammar of a bar chart, and a bar chart is the one thing a terrain system
must not be. Nine marks were drawn across four rounds. The rejection paragraph:

> **Contour chevrons** collapsed into an upload icon at 16px. **Dither tile**
> was noise at 16px, not a mark. **Even bars with a lichen cap** read as a
> striped square with no focal point. **Bars cut on a diagonal** read as signal
> strength. **A right-aligned summit** — the left ends tracing a ridge in
> negative space — was the best idea of the round and went muddy below 32px.
> **Nested contour diamonds** passed 16px and read as a thin wireframe standing
> next to eight solid, blocky siblings; it was the right idea at the wrong
> weight. **A sheared stack descending** read as falling. **A slab ridge** was a
> mountain, which is a photo icon. The kept mark is **a sheared stack climbing**:
> seven strokes of equal length, each nudged right as it climbs.

Equal lengths are the point. Nothing is plotted, so nothing can be misread as
data. What changes is the offset, and in a real hachure the strokes are drawn
down the line of steepest slope — so a sheared stack *is* a slope, and the lean
is its aspect. The lichen cap stayed where the apex diamond was, so the accent
did not move; only the grammar did.

Two corrections fell out of drawing it:

- **The channel is now a token.** It is `2.2` on the 64 grid, the same gap the
  dither leaves between cells, so the mark and the field are ruled alike. The
  old `5.3` was a number nobody could quote.
- **The mark has clear space.** The first v3.0 drawing ran to the edge of its
  own box, which made it optically larger than the wordmark and impossible to
  align with anything. It now sits in a channel and a half on every side, and
  the lockup draws it at 0.86 of the lockup size so its ink lands at 1.9 × the
  wordmark's cap height — where the drawn `lockup.svg` has it.

The cap colour was also drifting: the React component hard-coded `#B8CC3B`, a
lime that exists in no token. It now reads `--hx-sheet` (`#C9D94E`), verified in
the browser as `rgb(201, 217, 78)`.

### The field

The hero was a framed plate: a two-column grid with a bordered sheet on the
right. That is a picture of the system, not the system. The field is now the
hero's own band, full viewport width, with the copy on paper above it and a
printed margin below; the footer returns the same field at the page's bottom
edge, seeded differently and left to run off the sheet.

**The first attempt failed and is worth recording.** The field was stretched
behind the copy as a background layer. `drawRange` puts the highest crest at 16%
of its box, so the terrain rose straight into the lead paragraph and the accent
line went lichen-on-green — a contrast failure a class-name check would never
have caught. The field is now a **row of its own** in the hero's flex column. A
row cannot collide with the type at any width, which is a stronger guarantee
than tuning a fade until it happens to clear the last line.

What the frame lost, the margin kept: sheet number, scale bar, ratio and
coordinates are printed on the densest tone (`--hx-tone-7`), measured at 12.98:1
for the strip and 8.20:1 for the muted items. At night tone 7 is the *lightest*
step, so following it would have put a pale bar across a dark page; the margin
falls back to `--hx-surface-alt` and measures 14.95:1 / 6.66:1.

The engine gained one prop it needed and one it did not have: `develop` (stop a
range anywhere between paper and full tone), `fadeFrom`, and a theme watcher —
a canvas cannot inherit a CSS variable, so the field observes `data-theme` and
swaps ramp rather than painting a bright sheet across a dark page.

### Verified

`scripts/verify-hachure.mjs` now asserts pixels, not class names: both fields
are non-blank, full-bleed at 1440, have 6–7 distinct tones and darken downward;
the night ramp repaints the canvas; the margin's contrast is computed from
composited colours in both themes; the mark resolves to two paths with the cap
on the sheet token. `scrollWidth === clientWidth` at 1440, 1024, 768 and 390.
No console errors.

## v4.0 — the mark reset

The v3.0 mark was argued well and still read weak. The argument was that equal
strokes with a climbing offset encode slope without plotting data — true, and
the reason it survived a bake-off. What the argument missed is that a mark is
judged in a row, not in isolation.

Rendering all eight sibling marks at 112px settles it. `cove`, `huginn`,
`ledger`, `porphyry`, `strand` and `vitrine` are solid, saturated app-icon
tiles. `riso` is a two-plane paper collage. `clearing` is the one free-standing
line drawing. Six solid masses, one collage, one drawing — and Hachure, a stack
of hairlines. At 46px the seven-stroke mark is a list icon standing next to a
set of blocks. That is a weight failure, not a concept failure, and no amount of
shear tuning fixes it.

So the mass came back and the hachure moved into the silhouette. Nine marks were
drawn across three rounds, in two directions:

**The ramps** — strokes of equal length with thickness climbing, in three
weights, plus one flipped so the heavy stroke sat at the base. All four lost for
the same reason: **flip the drawing and the meaning flips**. Heavy at the summit
reads as a lit crest; heavy at the base reads as bedrock under a cap. A mark
whose orientation reverses its reading is a diagram, not an identity. The other
five of the round lost on sight: a **stepped silhouette** was a staircase, **one
diagonal stroke** was a pen, **two map-key bars** were a bar chart with a legend,
a **quantised right edge** was a torn sheet of paper, a **banded solid** was five
stripes and still a list icon, and a **thick contour arc** was a rainbow.

The rejection paragraph:

> **Stepped silhouette** read as a staircase at 16px. **One diagonal stroke**
> read as a pen nib. **Two map-key bars** read as a bar chart with a legend.
> **A quantised right edge** read as a torn sheet of paper. **A banded solid**
> was five stripes — the same list-icon failure at a heavier weight. **A thick
> contour arc** was a rainbow. **The weight ramp**, in three weights, was the
> best of the round and lost on a technicality that matters: reversed, it says
> the opposite thing. **Heavy base with a lit summit** was the same ramp flipped,
> and read as bedrock under a cap. The kept mark is **a solid mass with a lit
> crest**: one section through rising ground, the crest a band cut from the top
> edge so the accent is the ground and not a stripe laid on it.

Two things fell out of the reset:

- **The compact mark is gone.** It existed because seven 2.2-unit channels
  closed into a block below 24px. This drawing has no channel to close, so
  `mark-compact.svg` was deleted and the `compact` prop removed from
  `HachureMark`. A second drawing would have been the same bytes.
- **The lockup factor moved from 0.86 to 0.95.** The old ink was 58 × 58 of the
  64 grid; the new ink is 58 × 52. Holding 0.86 would have shrunk the mark
  against the wordmark by the height it lost, so the factor was raised to keep
  the ink at 1.4 × the wordmark height — where the drawn `lockup.svg` has it.

## Not verified

- **Print.** The palette is a screen approximation of a two-ink survey sheet;
  halftone at 2px, paper absorption and the real lichen ink have not been
  proofed. The board is a screen render of a paper artefact.
- **The altimeter's number** is a generated elevation, not a surveyed one.
- **Safari.** The component's `backdrop-filter` badge was not checked in Safari.
- **Screen reader.** Focus and ARIA were checked structurally, not with
  VoiceOver/NVDA.
- **Performance.** No Lighthouse/INP run. Four canvases draw on this route: the
  hero field, the footer field, the study and the range thumb. The two fields
  are now much larger than the plate they replaced (720 × 160 cells at 1440),
  so their develop is throttled to one step every 33ms instead of every frame,
  and both stop once finished. The study redraws only on control change or
  resize. The throttle is a judgement, not a measurement — no frame trace was
  taken.
- **The board's two plates** use a mirrored copy of the ridge maths because the
  board is a standalone HTML file. `features/hachure/dither-field.ts` and the component
  remain the source of truth; a change to the tone formula must be mirrored in
  `design/brand/hachure/brandkit.html`.
- **E2E** was skipped as instructed (`npm run test:e2e` not run).
- **Automated axe/Lighthouse** was not run; contrast is computed, focus is
  structured, but there is no automated accessibility scan in CI.
