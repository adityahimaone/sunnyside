Two inks.
One horizon.

Vesper is a design system for things used after dark — on-call consoles, ledgers, documentation nobody reads at noon. It prints in cobalt and cream, puts a star on the horizon, and refuses every glow the last five years taught us to add.

Read the idea Copy the tokens
01Premise

A design system is a horizon, not a spotlight.

A spotlight lights one thing and blinds you to the rest. A horizon tells you where the ground ends, from anywhere on it — and it is the only line in the artwork that never moves. Vesper is built from that line: one bar, one star resting on it, two inks, and nothing that glows.

Belief

Interface light should be printed, not emitted. If a surface cannot be reproduced on uncoated stock with two passes, it is decoration.

Enemy

The AI dashboard: a frosted pane floating over a violet gradient, every edge lit, no horizon anywhere. Expensive-looking, and it never says where you are.

Material

Cobalt and cream on uncoated stock, with a pale blue second impression that only appears where the press slipped.

Reject it when

Anyone can say “it looks like every other AI product and I cannot tell which page I am on.” That sentence is the whole test.

Two flat inks. No gradient, no glow.

Because
A gradient is a photograph of light. A second flat ink is a print of it — and it survives a photocopy, a screenshot and a cheap projector.
Not when
A photograph is the subject, not the ground.
Breaks next
Someone adds a radial gradient to a hero “for depth”. Depth is not a brand.

The horizon is a filled bar, never a stroke.

Because
A 1px stroke disappears at 16px and at eight metres. A 4-unit bar holds its weight at both, and it is what the mark stands on.
Not when
The rule belongs to a document — then it is a hairline, not the horizon.
Breaks next
A border creeps onto the mark, and the bar becomes a box.

The star is never redrawn, only re-inked.

Because
The geometry is fixed at 64 units. A theme flip swaps which of the two files is used — ink or cream — and never the drawing, which is why the reverse ships as a separate file.
Not when
The mark sits on a photograph: use mark-reverse at full opacity, not a blend mode.
Breaks next
`filter: invert()` on a logo — it inverts the anti-aliasing too — or a “responsive” mark that redraws itself.

Square plates. One pill, and only for navigation.

Because
A printed plate has corners. The pill is the one surface that is not printed — it floats, so it curves.
Not when
A touch target falls under 44px, where a square reads as a miss.
Breaks next
Every card rounded to 12px “to feel modern”, and the pill stops meaning anything.
02The mark

The star came down and stayed.

Eleven concepts were drawn across two rounds on one grid. The first six all lost for the same reason: they stood on a plinth, and a head on a plinth is a trophy. Round two dropped the plinth and replaced it with the horizon. The winner is a star 46 units wide and 40 tall, whose lower point lands exactly on a 44 × 4 bar.

Clear space

One horizon-bar height on all four sides — 4 units at the 64 grid. The bar is the unit; nothing else measures the mark.

Never

No gradient fill, no drop shadow, no outline, no rotation, no filter: invert() on the reverse file. The star is printed, not rendered.

Below 24px

Use the compact — a different drawing, not a scale: fatter arms and a 5-unit bar so the silhouette survives 16px.

Eleven concepts, scored.

Each concept was rasterised at 128, 40 and 16 in a browser and measured: ink coverage at 16px, how many separate pieces survive there, how much of its ink it keeps between 40 and 16, whether parts merge, centroid offset, grid use, and overlap with every other concept. Eight pass-or-fail checks, no weights. Horizon Star ranks 4 of 11 — two concepts survive downscaling better and lost anyway, because a scorer can measure whether a shape holds at 16px and cannot measure whether it says lamp.

04Surfaces

Six surfaces were tried. Four survived.

The artwork is a two-ink print, so every surface here has to survive being printed. Glass was tried first and rejected: a 14px blur turns 60,000 stipple marks into a smudge, and the stipple is the argument. The rejected two stay on the page so nobody re-proposes them in six months.

Stipple fieldShipped — the hero

340 streamlines leave one point above the lamp, each curving the same way, drawn as 60,000 cream ticks at 0.6–1.4px. Density falls with radius, so the light has a direction instead of a vignette. Generated as a raster: the same texture in SVG would be 60,000 elements.

Ordered ditherShipped — section dividers

A 4 × 4 Bayer matrix over a cobalt-to-cream ramp. Where the system needs a fade, it prints one: 16 thresholds, two inks, no blur. It reads as a gradient at arm’s length and as dots at 30cm, which is the point.

VesperVesper
Register ghostShipped — display type only

A 0.055em second impression of display type in the cream ink, as a text-shadow. It is the misregistration of a two-pass press, kept to display sizes: at 18px it reads as a mistake, at 66px as a decision.

Frosted paneRejected

Tried first, because the reference does it well: a 14px backdrop blur over the field with a 1px cream rim. On a stipple it turns 60,000 marks into a smudge — the texture the whole system is built on is exactly what a blur destroys. It is shown here so nobody re-proposes it.

Ruled ledgerShipped — the token sheet

A 1px rule every 32px in `line-subtle`, with the cream wash laid over it. Every table in the system sits on this: the rule is the plate, the row is the object, and the outer padding is removed rather than the inner.

Torn, not cut
Cut edgeRejected

A clip-path with 2–4% amplitude teeth. It reads as paper, but this system is printed, not torn: every edge in the artwork is a clean cut, and a torn edge would be a second metaphor competing with the horizon.

Reading · 18 / 1.62 / 66ch

The lamp is not the point. The point is that you can see four feet of the path in front of you, and that the next four feet will be lit by the time you get there. A design system that promises more than that is selling a spotlight.

So the type is set for reading at night: 18px, 1.62 leading, and a measure that stops at 66 characters — because a line that runs to 90 is a line you re-read twice.

Known gap · the field is a raster

The stipple is a 1600 × 900 PNG, 883 KB. It is generated, so it can be regenerated at any size, but it will not scale to a 5K hero without a second render.

Known gap · no dark field

The night scope flips the page, but the field asset stays cobalt. A true night field would need a second render with the inks swapped, and that has not been done.

Known gap · one weight of display

Fraunces is loaded as a variable font from 300 to 700, but the specimens only use 300 and 400. The heavier end is untested at display size.

Known gap · cast is unlicensed for product

The six companion marks are drawn for this system and free to use inside it. They have not been checked against existing trademarks.

Known gap · the scorer cannot see meaning

It measures whether a shape survives 16px, not whether it says lamp. Horn and Post top the table on survival and lost the bake-off on reading. That judgement is not in the table and is not pretended to be.

Known gap · one flag left standing

The shipped mark's mass sits 8.4% below centre. Its ink box is centred on the grid (x 9–55, y 8–56), so it seats correctly in a slot, and the weight is low because the horizon is at the bottom — but the flag is published rather than waived.
