Vitrine — five ideas, one material.

Same palette and theme DNA; only the family changes. Judge at 16px first. All five pass score-mark.py; the choice is silhouette and meaning, not a defect. One kept, four lost with reasons.

01 Architectural monogram

Bracket monogram

Lost

Huginn owns the silhouette. Repeating the two-bracket keystone makes the library read as one family, not five brands.

Argument: “A pair of brackets holds one key.” It is true, but it is already Huginn's sentence.

02 Offset geometric planes

Sheared sheets

Lost

Riso owns the overlapping sheet. The mark reads as a misregistration effect before it reads as a case.

Argument: “Two passes never quite agree.” That is Riso's sentence, not Vitrine's.

03 Brutalist louvers

Wedge bars

Lost

Hachure owns the stepped ridge. A louver is a shelf, not a vitrine; at 16px it becomes a bar chart.

Argument: “Relief is density.” True of Hachure's survey paper, not of a glass case.

04 Horizon & counter-form

Shelf and keystone

Lost

It collapses into Hachure at 16px. The bar and diamond share a silhouette, and the baseline carries no letter or case.

Argument: “A shelf holds one object.” The idea is right; the silhouette is already taken.

05 Continuous ribbon

Open case

Kept

One case, one plinth, one label. The open top is the glass; the plinth carries the printed label; the U silhouette is open, architectural and unused in the library.

Argument: “The case is open; the label is the only print.” The compact drops the label and keeps the heavier U.
